## 3. Material criteria and their propositions

### C1. `research-020-c1`

**Criterion:** States the Evidence Code section 917(a) presumption: where a privilege is claimed on the ground the matter is a confidential communication in a listed relationship, including the lawyer-client relationship, the communication is presumed to have been made in confidence and the opponent of the claim bears the burden of proof to establish it was not confidential.

#### Proposition `research-020-p1`

> If a privilege is claimed on the ground that the matter sought to be disclosed is a communication made in confidence in the course of the lawyer-client relationship (among other listed relationships), the communication is presumed to have been made in confidence and the opponent of the claim of privilege has the burden of proof to establish that the communication was not confidential.

**Verbatim authoritative excerpt relied on:**

```text
the communication is presumed to have been made in confidence and the opponent of the claim of privilege has the burden of proof to establish that the communication was not confidential.
```

### C3. `research-020-c3`

**Criterion:** Cites Costco Wholesale Corp. v. Superior Court (2009) 47 Cal.4th 725 for the holding that where the communication is a confidential one between attorney and client, the entire communication, including its recitation or summary of factual material, is privileged.

#### Proposition `research-020-p3`

> Costco Wholesale Corp. v. Superior Court (2009) 47 Cal.4th 725 states that when the communication is a confidential one between attorney and client, the entire communication, including its recitation or summary of factual material, is privileged.

**Verbatim authoritative excerpt relied on:**

```text
when the communication is a confidential one between attorney and client, the entire communication, including its recitation or summary of factual material, is privileged
```

```text
In this case we consider whether the trial court erred by directing a referee to conduct an in camera review of an opinion letter sent by outside counsel to a corporate client, allowing the referee to redact the letter to conceal that portion the referee believed to be privileged, and ordering the client to disclose the remainder to the opposing party.
```
