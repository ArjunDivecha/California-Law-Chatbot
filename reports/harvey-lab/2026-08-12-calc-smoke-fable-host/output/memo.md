## I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The data room reveals a pattern of issues falling into three broad groups:

**1. The earnings base supporting the $182M–$205M valuation is unreliable.** The single most important finding of this review is that the Company's EBITDA presentation is internally inconsistent and, in at least one instance, contradicted by the Company's own litigation disclosures. Management's "reported EBITDA" of $18.4M for FY2024 appears to equal operating income (EBIT), while the Company's own P&L arithmetic produces reported EBITDA of $26.0M — and the FY2022/FY2023 comparatives on the EBITDA bridge are computed on the other basis, obscuring what is in fact a **27% year-over-year decline in reported EBITDA** (FY2023: $25.2M → FY2024: $18.4M as presented). The detailed adjustment schedule produces Adjusted EBITDA of **$23.8M**, not the **$22.8M** in the CIM and LOI — an unexplained $1.0M gap management has acknowledged but not reconciled. The largest single add-back (the $1.2M "litigation settlement") is described three different, mutually exclusive ways across three documents — including as a settlement *receipt* (which would require a *downward* adjustment, a potential $2.4M swing). Under the bank's credit-agreement definition, EBITDA is only **$18.4M**; at the LOI's 8.0x–9.0x multiple range, every $1M of disallowed add-back is worth $8M–$9M of enterprise value. If the QoE lands near the bank definition, the implied valuation gap is roughly **$35M–$50M**.

**2. Revenue durability is materially weaker than presented.** The DOE IDIQ contract (19.2% of revenue; $24.4M TTM) has only **$12.3M of ceiling remaining**, projected by the Company's own analysis to exhaust in **April–May 2025 — the target closing window** — creating a disclosed **~$12.1M FY2025 revenue headwind** that is nowhere reflected in the CIM's growth narrative. The DOE contract also carries a 30-day change-of-ownership notification requirement (Clause H-12) with termination-for-default as the stated consequence of non-compliance. Separately — and alarmingly — the two customer schedules in the data room contain **irreconcilably different top-20 customer lists** (only DOE and Consolidated Mining appear on both). At least one of these documents is wrong.

**3. Structural closing impediments and uninsured legacy liabilities.** Closing triggers mandatory prepayment of ~$39.2M of secured debt within five business days (no consent/waiver mechanism exists); the Salt Lake City transfer station — supporting ~$16.2M of annual revenue — sits on an unconsented lease assignment *and* a hazardous waste permit still held by a subsidiary the Company's own org chart describes as **dormant**; the Wyoming (Casper) permit has **expired** and operates on timely-renewal grace; a **union election petition** filed November 4, 2024 covering 62 field technicians is disclosed only in a footnote and directly contradicts the CIM's "entirely non-unionized" representation; the Grand Junction property carries legacy soil/groundwater contamination that is **expressly uninsured** (gradual-release exclusion), costed only in stale 2019 dollars, and excludes vapor-intrusion and off-site exposure; and the discontinued asbestos abatement line has **no insurance coverage at all** (blanket CGL exclusion; no tail purchased) despite two open workers' compensation claims alleging respiratory exposure — claims whose existence contradicts the litigation summary's statement that no asbestos claims have been asserted.

Aggregate identified/quantifiable exposures (excluding valuation impact) include: debt payoff ~$39.2M (funded at closing); change-of-control severance up to $3.775M (and possibly ~$4.65M on one reading of the Calloway agreements); CDPHE penalty $0.2M–$1.16M (unaccrued); Ramirez litigation $0.5M–$4.8M (coverage uncertain); wage claims ~$0.33M; remediation $1.8M–$2.6M in 2019 dollars plus unquantified vapor-intrusion/off-site risk against a $2.2M accrual; deferred fleet replacement backlog ~$9.8M; and a ~$12.1M annual DOE revenue cliff.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed to signing at the current valuation until (i) the QoE resolves the EBITDA presentation and the customer-data conflict, (ii) an updated Phase II ESA and vapor-intrusion assessment are commissioned, and (iii) the consent/permit path for SLC, Casper, and the DOE contract is mapped. The SPA should include the specific protections in Section XIII of this memorandum, including special escrows beyond the 10% general holdback.

## II. FINANCIAL / QUALITY OF EARNINGS RED FLAGS

### RF-01. EBITDA presentation is internally inconsistent; the valuation metric is unverified — **CRITICAL**

- The QoE data package (Historical P&L tab) shows FY2024 operating income (EBIT) of $18.4M and D&A of $7.6M, which arithmetically yields EBITDA of **$26.0M**. Yet management presents "Reported EBITDA" of **$18.4M** — i.e., the EBIT figure — as the starting point of the EBITDA bridge. The P&L tab itself concedes: "Management presents EBITDA excluding D&A add-back differently."
- The bridge's comparative years use the *other* convention (FY2022: $21.0M; FY2023: $25.2M = EBIT + D&A). On a consistent basis, reported EBITDA **declined ~27% in FY2024** — a fact absent from the CIM, which shows EBITDA for FY2024 only and presents an uninterrupted growth story.
- The adjustment schedule totals **$5.4M** of add-backs producing Adjusted EBITDA of **$23.8M**; the CIM and LOI use **$22.8M** with "approximately $4.4M" of adjustments. The QoE workbook itself flags the $1.0M discrepancy: *"The specific adjustment category causing the discrepancy has not been identified."*
- **Impact:** the entire $182M–$205M valuation rests on a multiple of a number that does not tie to the Company's own financial detail. At 8.0x–9.0x, each $1M of EBITDA ≈ $8M–$9M of enterprise value.
- **Action:** Bridgewater Forsyth must rebuild EBITDA from the audited trial balance; do not lock economics until reconciled. Preserve LOI Section 3.2 repricing flexibility.

### RF-02. The $1.2M "litigation settlement" add-back is contradicted three ways — **CRITICAL**

Three documents give three mutually exclusive accounts of the largest discrete FY2024 add-back:

1. **CIM (§VI.B):** a one-time litigation settlement *cost incurred* by the Company in FY2024.
2. **Litigation & Regulatory Summary (§IV.A):** the $1.2M referenced in the EBITDA adjustment schedule is the **Clearwater Logistics settlement — a payment RES *received*** in April 2024 (i.e., non-recurring *income*). If true, the proper QoE treatment is a $1.2M *deduction* from EBITDA, not an add-back — a potential **$2.4M swing** ($9.6M–$21.6M of enterprise value at the LOI multiples).
3. **QoE workbook (EBITDA Bridge tab):** a $1.2M settlement **paid** to *Harper Environmental* in Q3 FY2024 under Mesa County Case No. 2023-CV-1847 — but the Litigation Summary (dated December 12, 2024) states the Harper case is **still pending**, in discovery, with mediation scheduled for February 2025, and describes RES as the *plaintiff* seeking $2.1M. The QoE description also inverts the claim (describing Harper as claimant on an exclusivity clause).

**Action:** demand the underlying settlement agreement(s), bank records, and general ledger detail. This is both a QoE issue and a disclosure-reliability issue bearing on fraud/indemnity architecture.

### RF-03. Bank-defined EBITDA is $18.4M; $5.4M of management add-backs are expressly disallowed — **CRITICAL**

- The High Plains credit agreement defines Consolidated EBITDA to exclude precisely the categories management adds back: owner-comp normalization, discontinued-operations/wind-down costs, restructuring adjustments, and non-GAAP items; only $500K/year of extraordinary charges qualifies. Bank EBITDA: **~$18.4M**; leverage tested at 2.10x on that basis.
- A third-party lender to the buyout will likely underwrite closer to the bank definition than to management's $22.8M/$23.8M. At 8.5x, the difference between $18.4M and $22.8M is **~$37M of enterprise value**.
- **Action:** treat the credit-agreement definition as the anchor for debt capacity modeling; pressure-test every add-back (see RF-04–RF-06).

### RF-04. Individual add-backs are aggressive or demonstrably recurring — **HIGH**

- **Owner compensation normalization ($950K/yr):** computed off base salaries only ($875K × 2 → $400K × 2), but FY2024 actual total owner compensation was ~$2.19M including bonuses; the add-back is claimed identically in FY2022–FY2024. The Calloways' evergreen employment agreements remain at $875K base — the "market rate" replacement cost is hypothetical unless the buyer renegotiates their contracts at closing.
- **ERP implementation ($650K, plus $385K in FY2023):** the CIM states the system is "fully operational and no further implementation expenditures are anticipated," but the QoE workbook states **Phase 2 is not expected to be complete until Q2 FY2025**. The cost is continuing, not one-time. (Note also that RidgeTrack development is simultaneously *capitalized* in growth capex — $2.3M over five years — warranting a capitalization-policy review.)
- **Pinnacle integration ($800K in FY2024, after $1.24M and $620K in FY2022–23):** third consecutive year of an "integration" add-back for an acquisition management calls "substantially complete"; smells like run-rate cost.
- **Asbestos wind-down ($1.8M in FY2024 + $920K in FY2023):** legitimate direction, but the service line was revenue-generating through FY2023 ($3.4M revenue) and the add-back schedule does not net historical asbestos revenue/losses consistently; FY2023 adjusted figures embed the same issue.

### RF-05. Historical financials do not tie across data room documents — **HIGH**

- Revenue: FY2021 is $82.1M (CIM/P&L) vs. **$78.9M** (customer contract summary); FY2022 is $97.6M (CIM) vs. $97.8M (P&L) vs. **$98.2M** (customer summary); FY2023 is $114.8M (CIM) vs. $114.6M (P&L) vs. **$115.7M** (customer summary).
- Gross profit/margin: CIM shows FY2020 GP of $23.1M (33.8%); the P&L shows $23.9M (35.0%). Similar deltas in each year.
- **Audit status is represented three inconsistent ways:** the CIM says the financial statements "have not been audited" and are "reviewed by Hansen & Whitfield, CPAs"; the QoE cover sheet says FY2020–FY2023 were "audited by Plante Moran PLLC" with FY2024 reviewed; the Litigation Summary references an ASC 450 footnote in "the FY2024 audited financial statements." The LOI (§8(i)) makes delivery of **audited FY2022–FY2024 financials** a closing condition — if no audits exist, that condition may be unsatisfiable on the deal timeline.
- **Action:** obtain audit opinions (if any) and engagement letters; reconcile all revenue schedules; condition signing on audited or auditable financials.

### RF-06. Maintenance capex is understated; ~$9.8M deferred fleet replacement backlog — **HIGH**

- Management normalizes maintenance capex at $4.0M–$4.5M/year. But the fleet schedule shows **38 of 85 units (44.7%) at or past stated useful life**, average fleet age 8.2 years vs. 7.0-year useful life, and an estimated **$9.8M replacement cost for past-life units alone**. The QoE workbook itself notes ~$3.3M/year of catch-up capex above run-rate for three years — implying true near-term maintenance capex of **~$7.5M+/year**, roughly 70% above management's normalized figure.
- The FY2022–FY2023 dip in truck replacement spend (to $1.2M from $2.1M) was a deliberate deferral during Pinnacle integration — i.e., recent EBITDA was flattered by under-maintenance.
- **Impact:** ~$3M+/year of additional true cost directly reduces free cash flow and debt capacity; also an asset-quality/condition rep issue.

### RF-07. 27% EBITDA decline and decelerating growth masked by presentation — **HIGH**

- On the P&L's own consistent figures, FY2024 reported EBITDA declined 27% YoY and EBITDA margin fell from 22.0% to 20.5% (or from 16.0% to 14.5% on the EBIT basis management labels EBITDA); revenue growth decelerated from ~20% to 11.1%. The CIM presents none of this trend data (it omits FY2020–FY2023 EBITDA entirely).

## III. REVENUE CONCENTRATION AND CUSTOMER CONTRACT RED FLAGS

### RF-08. DOE IDIQ ceiling exhaustion: ~$12.1M FY2025 revenue cliff at closing — **CRITICAL**

- DOE (19.2% of revenue; $24.4M TTM) is served under IDIQ DE-EM-0005523 with a $45M ceiling; **$32.7M (72.7%) is consumed with only 61.7% of the term elapsed**. Remaining ceiling: **$12.3M**. The Company's own utilization analysis projects **ceiling exhaustion in approximately April–May 2025 — precisely the target closing window** — and quantifies a **"$12.1M+ potential FY2025 revenue headwind"** (9.5% of total revenue) absent a ceiling increase or recompete win.
- The CIM affirmatively markets this contract as providing "substantial near-term revenue visibility" through August 2026 without disclosing the ceiling math — a material disclosure gap.
- **Action:** model FY2025/FY2026 with DOE revenue at ≤$12.3M; diligence the ceiling-increase/recompete pipeline; consider an earnout or purchase-price contingency tied to DOE renewal; verify no task orders have been issued in excess of remaining ceiling.

### RF-09. Two irreconcilable top-20 customer lists — **CRITICAL (data integrity)**

- `customer-contract-summary.xlsx` and the QoE workbook's "Revenue by Customer" tab present **materially different customer rosters** for the same FY2024 TTM period. Beyond DOE (#1) and Consolidated Mining (#2), the lists diverge entirely — e.g., #3 is "Western Slope Municipal Water District" ($9.8M) in one and "Western Slope Energy Partners" ($8.9M) in the other; one lists Intermountain Health, High Plains Energy, and Silver Creek Mining; the other lists Rio Tinto Kennecott, Suncor, Freeport-McMoRan, Marathon, and Newmont. Historical per-customer revenue also differs (e.g., CMMC FY2020: $8.2M vs. $8.55M).

- **Action:** demand invoice-level revenue registers and executed contracts for every top-20 name on both lists; make accuracy of customer schedules a specifically indemnified representation.

### RF-10. Change-of-control/assignment consents across the book of business — **HIGH**

- **DOE:** non-assignable (Anti-Assignment Act, 41 U.S.C. § 6305; FAR 42.12). Even for a stock deal, **Clause H-12 requires written notice to the Contracting Officer within 30 days of any change in ownership or control; failure "may be grounds for termination for default."** Novation analysis and CO engagement must precede closing.
- **8 of the top 20** contracts contain assignment/CoC/notification provisions (Intermountain Health — consent required + 60-day notice; Utah DEQ — termination right on ownership change; Rocky Mountain Pipeline — 30-day consent period; CDOT; Bridger Valley; Frontier E&P; Teton Steel; Summit County — county board approval).

- Five Pinnacle-legacy contracts (~$20.5M, incl. Wasatch Front Petroleum and Silver Creek Mining) run through the compromised SLC facility (see RF-17) — combined facility-dependent revenue ~$16.2M.

## IV. DEBT / CHANGE-OF-CONTROL RED FLAGS

### RF-11. Mandatory prepayment of ~$39.2M within 5 business days of closing; no waiver mechanism — **CRITICAL**

- The acquisition triggers the credit agreement's Change of Control definition (clauses (a) and (c)). Section 2.11(c) requires prepayment in full of all Obligations within **five business days**; the revolver commitment terminates and LCs must be cash-collateralized at 105%. Change of Control is an **immediate Event of Default with no cure period**, and the CFO's summary confirms **no consent or waiver mechanism exists** — payoff or refinancing is the only path.
- Estimated payoff (9/30/24 balances): $12.4M revolver + $26.3M term loan + **1.00% prepayment premium (~$263K, applicable to closings on or before January 16, 2026)** + accrued interest ($200K–$300K) + SOFR breakage ≈ **$39.2M–$39.3M**. Note the LOI's Net Debt definition captures principal and accrued interest but is silent on the prepayment premium and breakage — clarify allocation now.
- Term-loan amortization of $657.5K/quarter begins January 17, 2025; balances at an April 30, 2025 closing will differ from the data room figures.
- Ancillary EoD triggers relevant to the pre-closing period: Derek Calloway ceasing to be CEO without lender-approved successor; judgment >$1M not insured/bonded; **material environmental event or remediation order >$1M; revocation/non-renewal of any material permit** (see RF-18); material labor dispute >10 business days (see RF-27); 5-business-day notice obligations for material litigation, environmental events, and NLRB actions — confirm the Company gave required notices for the CDPHE NOV, Ramirez, and the NLRB petition.

### RF-12. Credit agreement date and lien inconsistencies — **HIGH**

- The org chart (§3.6) refers to a credit agreement dated **September 14, 2021**, and suggests Company **shares** may be encumbered under it; the credit agreement summary and CIM date the facility **January 17, 2023** and describe the collateral as Company assets plus Pinnacle equity (not RES shares). - **Action:** obtain complete loan files, UCC/lien searches, and confirm whether any predecessor facility, share pledge, or unreleased lien exists; run bring-down searches at closing.

## V. ENVIRONMENTAL RED FLAGS

### RF-13. Grand Junction legacy contamination: stale costing, uninsured, accrual likely inadequate — **CRITICAL**

- The June 2019 Phase II ESA confirms petroleum (TPH/BTEX) and chlorinated solvent (PCE/TCE/DCE) contamination in soil and groundwater exceeding CDPHE standards at the Company's owned HQ, with remediation estimated at **$1.8M–$2.6M in 2019 dollars**. The estimate **expressly excludes**: (i) vapor-intrusion assessment/mitigation, (ii) off-site groundwater remediation, (iii) third-party claims, and (iv) NRD claims — and warns DNAPL migration "could substantially increase" scope. **No updated ESA has been performed in 5.5 years**, despite Elkhorn's explicit recommendation to update prior to any transaction. The accrual attributable to Grand Junction is **$2.2M** — within the stale 2019 range, before inflation and exclusions.
- **Insurance does not respond:** the pollution legal liability policy **expressly excludes gradual releases at owned/operated facilities**, and the ESA characterizes all contamination as gradual, long-term releases. The Grand Junction remediation is effectively **self-insured**.
- The lender holds a mortgage on the property and received a reliance letter on the same 2019 ESA; a remediation order >$1M is an EoD under the credit agreement.
- As a stock buyer, Sycamore inherits owner/operator status under CERCLA/RCRA regardless of the contamination's pre-2009 origin (subject to defenses that require diligence — e.g., BFPP protections generally unavailable to a stock purchaser of the existing owner).
- **Action:** commission updated Phase II ESA and vapor-intrusion assessment *now*; obtain fresh remediation cost estimate in current dollars; negotiate a dedicated environmental escrow/indemnity (LOI §5.5 already contemplates special treatment) sized to the updated estimate plus contingency; evaluate CDPHE VCUP entry and environmental insurance (PLL tail per RF-24).

### RF-14. Vapor intrusion into occupied building — unassessed employee-exposure risk — **HIGH**

- 2019 soil gas results at SG-09/SG-10 exceeded EPA commercial RSLs for PCE (450 µg/m³ vs. 240) and TCE (120 µg/m³ vs. 21 — nearly 6x) adjacent to the 28,000 sq ft vehicle maintenance building **occupied daily by RES employees**. Elkhorn called follow-up "a matter warranting prompt attention." Five and a half years later, no sub-slab or indoor-air sampling has been performed.
- This is simultaneously: a potential OSHA/occupational exposure liability; a template for Ramirez-style personal injury claims (see RF-21); a possible mitigation capex item (sub-slab depressurization); and a disclosure problem.

### RF-15. Off-site plume migration never ruled out — **MEDIUM**

- Groundwater "containment" was labeled *preliminary* by Elkhorn; there are no off-site sentinel wells, flow is toward the Colorado River (1.2 miles), and confirmed off-site migration would trigger Colorado Reg. 41 notifications and "materially increase the scope, complexity, and cost" of remediation. Also note the permit schedule *mischaracterizes* the ESA (locating contamination in the "northern portion," identifying only TCE, and describing migration toward the north/east) — the ESA shows western petroleum and eastern solvent source areas with northwest flow. Another data-integrity data point.

### RF-16. CDPHE enforcement action: unaccrued penalty of $0.2M–$1.16M and inconsistent factual accounts — **HIGH**

- NOV CDPHE-HW-2024-0093 (July 18, 2024) at Grand Junction. Penalty range: Company position **$450K max** (12 days × $37,500); CDPHE position **$1,162,500** (31 days); management settlement estimate $200K–$450K. **No accrual recorded.** Settlement conference expected Q1 2025 — during the diligence/signing window.
- The data room gives **two different violation narratives**: the Litigation Summary describes ~14,000 gallons of F001/F002 solvents in an unpermitted staging area beyond the 90-day accumulation limit; the Permit Schedule describes a satellite-accumulation-area 55-gallon-limit issue. These are different violations with different penalty postures. Obtain the NOV itself and the September 5, 2024 response.
- The LOI (§5.5) already flags this docket for special indemnification — implement via a specific indemnity excluded from basket/cap, with an escrow holdback pending resolution.

### RF-17. Salt Lake City facility: unconsented lease assignment + permit held by a "dormant" entity — **CRITICAL**

Roughly **$16.2M of annual revenue** (12.7%) depends on a facility with two unresolved, compounding defects:

- **Lease:** the SLC lease (Wasatch Industrial Properties) deems any change of control of the tenant an assignment requiring landlord consent. Consent for the 2022 Pinnacle→RES change of control was requested April 12, 2022 and **never received** despite follow-ups. In **November 2024 the landlord sent a lease-compliance inquiry specifically invoking the consent-to-assignment clause** — this is not dormant; the landlord is aware and asking questions on the eve of a second change-of-control event (the Sycamore acquisition). A pre-existing default condition may exist.
- **Permit:** Utah permit UTH-0441 remains in **Pinnacle's name**; the transfer application has been pending for **2.5+ years**. Utah Admin. Code R315-3 requires Division approval of ownership/control modifications *before* they are effective. Meanwhile, the org chart states Pinnacle has been **dormant with no operations since Q3 2022** and the permit schedule confirms RES personnel operate the facility — i.e., the facility is arguably operated by a non-permittee. Management's comfort position (Pinnacle is a wholly-owned subsidiary) is in tension with its own dormancy disclosure. Note also Pinnacle's EPA transporter ID was deactivated while the facility permit stayed in its name — inconsistent treatment.
- **Action:** make landlord consent (curing both the 2022 assignment and the Sycamore CoC) and permit transfer/confirmation from the Utah DWM **conditions to closing**; obtain estoppel from Wasatch; quantify relocation cost/feasibility as leverage; specific indemnity for pre-closing operation-under-Pinnacle-permit exposure.

### RF-18. Casper, WY permit expired; operating on timely-renewal grace — **HIGH**

- Permit WY-HW-2019-033 **expired November 30, 2024**; renewal filed October 15, 2024 (46 days prior — confirm Wyoming's minimum filing window was met); the facility (68–75 employees; Wyoming = ~$30.2M of revenue) operates under timely-renewal provisions with agency action "expected Q1 2025." The CIM describes the permit portfolio without disclosing the expiration. Credit agreement lists permit non-renewal as an EoD. **Action:** closing condition that the renewed permit be issued (or written DEQ confirmation of continued authorization); verify renewal application completeness.

## VI. LITIGATION AND REGULATORY RED FLAGS

### RF-19. Ramirez v. RES: $4.8M toxic-exposure claim with unresolved coverage — **HIGH**

- Former field technician alleges 10 months of VOC/heavy-metal exposure (March 2023–January 2024) from inadequate PPE/air monitoring; claims include negligence per se (OSHA) and punitive damages; trial set February 2026 (inside the 18-month escrow window). Management estimates $0.5M–$1.5M; no accrual.
- **Coverage is doubly uncertain:** insurer issued a reservation of rights September 19, 2024, and the data room *disagrees with itself* about where the claim was tendered — Litigation Summary: CGL + **pollution** policies; Insurance Summary: CGL + **workers' compensation**. The CGL contains a standard pollution exclusion (VOC exposure claims are classic pollution-exclusion territory); employers' liability limits are only **$1M**; the PLL is claims-made and covers third-party job sites — but a 10-month exposure period may implicate gradual-condition arguments and the 72-hour reporting feature; punitive damages are generally uninsurable in many states. Net exposure could be substantially uninsured.
- The alleged conduct (failure to follow site H&S plan) also cuts against the CIM's safety narrative and — combined with the HAZWOPER certification gap at RF-28 — suggests systemic safety-compliance diligence is warranted.

### RF-20. Asbestos long-tail liability: uninsured, and disclosures conflict — **CRITICAL**

- RES performed asbestos abatement at **~145 sites (2012–2023)** generating ~$31.2M of revenue. The Litigation Summary asserts the current pollution policy provides completed-operations coverage from the January 1, 2015 retroactive date and states "**no such claims have been asserted to date**."
- Both statements are contradicted elsewhere: (i) the Insurance Summary discloses a **blanket asbestos exclusion on the CGL "regardless of when such exposure occurred,"** confirms completed-operations coverage for asbestos work is **excluded**, and states **no tail or extended completed-operations coverage has been procured**; the PLL summary nowhere affirms asbestos coverage; and (ii) the QoE workbook discloses **two open workers' compensation claims (filed 2021 and 2022) by former asbestos abatement employees alleging occupational respiratory exposure**.
- 2012–2014 operations predate the PLL retroactive date in any event. Latency for asbestos disease runs decades; there is **no reserve** and, on the better reading of the documents, **no insurance**.
- **Action:** insurance archaeology (locate all historical occurrence-based GL policies 2012–2023); obtain the two open WC claim files; evaluate standalone asbestos liability cover; negotiate a long-survival specific indemnity (the 6-year environmental survival in the LOI is likely too short for asbestos latency — seek longer or fundamental-style treatment) and consider excluding asbestos from the cap.

### RF-21. FLSA/COMPS wage demand (~$330K claimed) — **MEDIUM**

- Demand letter (October 2024) from eight former asbestos-line technicians: ~$165K unpaid overtime + equal liquidated damages. Management estimates $100K–$200K; no accrual; relies on the FLSA motor-carrier exemption — a fact-intensive defense, and note the **COMPS Order does not incorporate the federal motor-carrier exemption**, so the Colorado claims may survive that defense. Collective-action risk if filed. Treat as pre-closing liability/specific indemnity.

### RF-22. Harper counterclaim and litigation-schedule hygiene — **MEDIUM**

- Harper counterclaim ($340K) assessed "Remote" with no reserve; mediation February 2025 (pre-signing — monitor; a settlement *payment* would further confound RF-02). Aggregate unreserved contingent exposure per the Company: **$0.8M–$2.49M** (using the Company's own CDPHE position; +$712.5K if CDPHE's 31-day position prevails) — against $0 of accruals other than remediation.

## VII. INSURANCE RED FLAGS

### RF-23. Structural coverage gaps — **HIGH**

- **No umbrella over pollution:** the $25M umbrella sits over CGL/Auto/EL only; PLL is capped at $10M/occurrence, $15M aggregate, with **defense costs eroding limits** and a $1M regulatory-defense sublimit — thin for a hazmat business facing an active enforcement action and a $4.8M exposure suit.
- **Gradual-release exclusion at owned facilities** (see RF-13) leaves the flagship legacy liability uninsured.
- **Employers' liability at $1M** per accident/disease vs. a $4.8M employee exposure claim.
